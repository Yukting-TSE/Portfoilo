Abstract. Human-Centered Design (HCD) emphasizes user-centric research but 
faces inefﬁciencies and subjectivity in traditional methods. While AI enhances 
data processing in early and late research stages, existing tools remain passive, 
lacking real-time collaboration during interviews. This study explores AI’s active 
role in HCD through three questions: integrating AI into workﬂows, enhancing 
creativity, and deﬁning human-AI collaboration models. Semi-structured inter-
views with 12 researchers across industries highlight gaps in real-time interaction, 
insight depth, and multimodal data integration. Key design requirements include 
improving communication, data quality, analytical efﬁciency, and research trans-
lation. Participants expect AI agents to automate processes, support decisions, and 
adapt to domains. We propose Co-Researcher, an AI framework enabling real-time 
transcription, dynamic questioning, and contextual analysis. The system fosters 
co-creative interviews, with AI suggesting follow-ups and generating structured 
reports. Results show Co-Researcher reduces manual tasks and deepens insights, 
positioning AI as a collaborative partner rather than a tool. This work advances 
human-AI collaboration in HCD, balancing automation with creativity and rigor, 
while addressing cross-functional alignment and methodological challenges. 
Keywords: User Research · Human-AI Collaboration · Co-creative 
1 Introduction 
Human-Centered Design (HCD) [38, 39] emphasizes user needs, experiences, and expec-
tations, placing the user voice at the core of the design process to ensure alignment with 
actual demands. Unlike the “Technology Push” approach, HCD requires designers to 
integrate qualitative and quantitative research, continuously gathering user feedback to 
reﬁne design directions [ 2, 10]. 
However, current user research faces challenges such as high costs, low efﬁciency, 
and excessive subjectivity [ 34– 36]. With advancements in artiﬁcial intelligence (AI), 
language models like GPT4.0 have improved data processing efﬁciency, reducing time 
and labor costs [12, 15, 37]. AI, through natural language processing (NLP) and sentiment 
analysis, can automatically analyze user feedback, aiding designers in understanding 
user needs [ 48]. Y et, existing AI tools primarily support the early and late stages of

2 Related Work 
2.1 Design and User Research: Process and Roles 
Human-centered design emphasizes understanding user perspectives to generate effec-
tive solutions. Norman (2002) highlights the role of empathy in shaping user-oriented 
design strategies [ 8]. Brown (2009) further asserts that user research is integral to cre-
ative design, guiding solutions through deep analysis of user needs, behaviors, and 
emotions [41]. 
Methodologically, Buchanan (2001) regards user research as a sub-design process, 
with the Double Diamond model providing a structured framework [12, 15]. This model, 
deﬁned by the Design Council (2005), consists of discovery, deﬁnition, development, 
and delivery phases, where user research is critical in the ﬁrst two stages. Effective 
user research requires both divergent and convergent thinking, involving skills such as 
creativity, empathy, critical thinking, data analysis, and communication [ 6, 11]. 
The user research process typically includes deﬁning interview objectives, planning, 
execution, data recording, and analysis (Snyder, 2003) [ 50]. Creativity plays a role at 
each stage: brainstorming interview questions, adapting during interviews, and deriv-
ing insights from data. Patton (2002) emphasizes that creativity extends beyond idea 
generation to pattern recognition and extracting meaningful insights from user feedback 
[51]. Kuniavsky (2003) underscores that user research creativity manifests in designing 
interview questions, data collection, and translating complex behaviors into actionable 
design insights [52]. 
However, traditional methods such as interviews, observations, and surveys remain 
labor-intensive and inefﬁcient, especially in dynamic design environments (Kuniavsky, 
2003) [2, 14, 52].

2.2 Applications of AI in User Research 
AI has been increasingly applied to user research, signiﬁcantly improving efﬁciency and 
depth of analysis. 
Problem Identiﬁcation. AI aids in identifying patterns and problems, traditionally 
reliant on researcher expertise. Brynjolfsson and McAfee (2014) suggest AI-driven 
processes enhance data-driven decision-making [ 53]. ChatGPT leverages text mining 
and semantic analysis to extract recurring themes and pain points from vast datasets, 
assisting designers in uncovering latent needs [ 10, 19]. Brainstorm Buddy [ 55] facili-
tates AI-driven brainstorming by generating user personas and reﬁning key questions. 
Shmueli (2010) notes that AI predictive analysis can preemptively identify emerging 
trends, providing designers with proactive insights [ 8, 23, 54]. 
Data Analysis and Synthesis. AI also excels in post-research data processing. Tools 
like Tencent Meeting Assistant [56] analyze meeting content and participant sentiment, 
while Feishu Miaoji automates transcription and key point extraction, aiding rapid data 
organization [ 57]. Davenport (2018) explores generative AI’s role in simulating user 
scenarios and generating design concepts [ 58]. GAN-based tools can create user per-
sonas and hypothetical scenarios, broadening the scope of design exploration [ 3, 19]. 
Additionally, AI-driven concept similarity generation expands creative possibilities for 
designers [5, 8]. 
AI-Driven Real-Time Collaboration. AI fosters real-time collaboration in user 
research. McCormack et al. (2020) demonstrate AI’s ability to co-create with human 
users in dynamic, interactive processes [ 49]. NLP and speech recognition technolo-
gies enhance real-time feedback analysis. Predictive models identify latent user needs, 
particularly in personalized services and product design [ 10, 25]. Platforms like IBM 
Watson and Google Cloud NLP use sentiment analysis and topic extraction to quickly 
highlight key user feedback insights, improving analytical depth and efﬁciency [ 24, 27]. 
Recent advancements have introduced AI agents into user research. Kusal (2022) demon-
strates how conversational AI integrates contextual awareness, emotions, and sentiment 
to simulate human behavior, improving designers’ understanding of user needs [ 59]. 
Despite AI’s potential, existing studies predominantly focus on post-research data 
processing. The role of AI agents in active collaboration during user research remains 
underexplored, presenting opportunities for further innovation.

This study employs semi-structured interviews as the primary research method to gather 
insights into participants’ experiences with user research processes and the potential 
role of AI agents in enhancing creativity. The semi-structured approach enables in-
depth discussions while allowing ﬂexibility for participants to share insights beyond 
predeﬁned questions.

3.2 Interview Structure 
To explore AI’s potential in supporting user research, we conducted semi-structured 
interviews, each lasting 1–2 h. Participants were asked the following questions: 
Q1: Can you brieﬂy describe your typical user research process? What are the key 
objectives at each stage? 
Q2: What types of information are most critical in your research? How do you 
differentiate between qualitative and quantitative data? 
Q3: Beyond interviewees, which other roles (e.g., domain experts) are crucial in 
supporting user research? Why? 
Q4: Have you used AI tools in user research? If so, in which stages were they 
beneﬁcial, and what were the outcomes? 
Q5: What are the main challenges you encounter in user research? In what ways do 
you think AI could help? 
Q6: If you could design an ideal AI-assisted tool for user research, what key features 
and functionalities would you expect? 
This structured yet ﬂexible interview approach allowed us to gain a deeper under-
standing of the research landscape and the potential integration of AI agents into design 
revision tools.

4.2 Existing User Research Collaboration Models 
User researchers navigate complex cross-team collaborations and face multiple chal-
lenges, from requirement clariﬁcation to data analysis and research outcome translation. 
Based on interview data, we categorized key pain points into four dimensions: ambiguous 
requirements and communication gaps, participant recruitment and data quality bottle-
necks, inefﬁciencies in analysis and insight extraction, and barriers to research-to-design 
translation. These challenges inform core design requirements (DRs) for future AI tools 
(Table 1). 
Across the user research workﬂow, challenges arise in requirement communication, 
participant recruitment, data processing, and insight implementation. Future AI tools 
should extend beyond data processing to address demand clariﬁcation, cross-team com-
munication, intelligent data analysis, and knowledge management, thereby enhancing 
research efﬁciency and impact. 
4.3 Design Expectations for AI Agents in User Research 
Based on interview analyses, we distilled key user expectations for AI agents in user 
research into four core dimensions: process automation, intelligent decision support, 
cross-role collaboration, and privacy control and domain specialization (Table 2). 
The core design requirements (DRs) in current workﬂows emphasize clarity in 
requirement articulation, data quality, and research-to-design translation. Meanwhile, 
user expectations (DEs) for AI agents focus on automation, intelligence, and domain 
adaptation. While participants from different ﬁelds (e.g., psychology expert P10 vs. gam-
ing researcher P7) exhibit varying needs, a shared expectation remains: AI should serve 
as an assistive rather than a replacement tool, enhancing user research comprehensively 
while maintaining control and interpretability.

5.2 User Interaction Prototype of Co-researcher 
To enhance the collaborative efﬁciency between designers and artiﬁcial intelligence 
(AI) agents, this study presents the development of an interactive prototype called Co-
Researcher. The system is designed to optimize the interview recording, analysis, and 
feedback processes, aiming to improve the quality and efﬁciency of research work while 
assisting designers in thoroughly exploring user needs. Co-Researcher’s user interface 
integrates high-precision speech-to-text technology, an AI-assisted question-generation 
system, and automated content organization tools. (Fig. 4). 
Fig. 4. User Interaction Prototype. 
F1: Real-time Speech-to-Text Recording. Co-Researcher employs high-precision 
speech recognition technology to transcribe spoken content into a structured verba-
tim transcript in real time. This feature not only enhances recording efﬁciency but also 
minimizes human error, ensuring the completeness and traceability of interview content. 
F2: AI Proactive Questioning Aids. The system is equipped with an intelligent Dia-
logue component, allowing researchers to interact with the AI agent to generate more 
targeted questions. The AI analyzes respondents’ answers in real time and automatically 
generates follow-up questions to deepen the interview dialogue, ensuring continuity 
while helping to uncover latent user needs. 
F3: Real-time Annotation and Extraction. Researchers can annotate the transcript in 
real time, highlighting and extracting key content. This functionality streamlines infor-
mation extraction, reducing redundant communication among team members caused by 
information discrepancies (Fig. 5). 
Co-Researcher divides the interview process into three main stages: preparation, 
interview, and data analysis. Each stage is focused on improving efﬁciency and reducing 
information gaps, ensuring that the research is completed effectively and accurately.

5.3 System Design 
This study presents the design and implementation of a comprehensive multimodal data 
processing and user interaction system, structured to support research tasks efﬁciently. 
The system adopts a modular architecture, encompassing four sequential stages—Input, 
Encoding, Processing, and Output—to facilitate data collection, analysis, and visualiza-
tion. The core objective is to transform raw multimodal data into high-value insights 
and present them through an intuitive and interactive interface, thereby enhancing the 
efﬁciency of User research. (Fig. 6). 
Fig. 6. Pipeline. 
Input. The system’s frontend is developed using React, which manages user interactions 
and task assignments while seamlessly integrating with a Flask backend via API calls. 
Real-time audio input is captured through WebRTC, enabling speech recording and 
multimodal data acquisition relevant to the research topic. Additionally, the system 
incorporates LLMLingua to support multilingual processing, ensuring compatibility 
across diverse linguistic environments. To enhance usability, a task reminder mechanism 
is embedded within the interface, guiding users through the data collection process 
efﬁciently. 
Encoding. The encoding phase focuses on text preprocessing, transformation, and fea-
ture extraction. Raw audio data undergoes transcription via OpenAI Whisper, generat-
ing textual content alongside timestamps and conﬁdence scores. The system employs 
Pandas for structured data organization, facilitating subsequent computational anal-
ysis. Further, BERT is utilized for text embedding, converting textual information 
into high-dimensional vector representations. These embeddings are processed through 
MLP/SVM classiﬁers, applying Softmax to compute probability distributions and gen-
erate classiﬁcation results with enhanced accuracy. By structuring and ﬁltering data 
effectively, this stage ensures a robust foundation for downstream analytical tasks.

6 Discussion 
In recent advancements in human-AI collaboration, the focus has shifted from AI as a 
mere tool to a more collaborative and creative agent. However, existing human-computer 
interaction (HCI) frameworks often limit AI’s role to supporting tasks, primarily per-
forming repetitive work that substitutes human effort. In creative domains, AI tends to 
passively mimic human creativity during the generation process. The question of how 
AI, with its distinct creativity, can actively engage in human-driven creative processes, 
facilitating co-creation, remains an underexplored topic. 
Within the context of Human-Centered Design (HCD) user research, we propose 
a framework for real-time human-AI collaboration. The AI co-researcher, an example 
within this framework, demonstrated an enhancement in both designer experience and 
creativity. AI, in this context, assumes a more active role in collaboration, not merely as a 
tool but as a creative partner. Our research provides insights for future design directions 
in AI-powered tools. 
6.1 AI’s Role in Collaborative Creation 
As discussed earlier, AI can stimulate human creativity in various ways during co-
creation: offering unexpected ideas, enhancing designers’ metacognitive skills, and gen-
erating collective intelligence. We identify three speciﬁc roles of AI as a co-researcher: 
AI as an expert, AI as a team member, and AI as an insight proposer. These roles high-
light the diverse ways AI can inspire creativity by complementing human abilities in 
ways that are both fundamentally distinct and mutually enriching. AI introduces eas-
ily digestible external knowledge, which designers can absorb as collective insights, 
and it also offers novel interpretations of existing content, providing fresh perspectives 
that inspire individual creativity. These two modes of AI collaboration often coexist. In 
design, real-time adjustments should be made based on the context to meet user needs, 
such as allowing designers to personalize AI parameters.

6.2 Limitations and Future Work 
Despite the promising results, our study has certain limitations. One key challenge lies 
in addressing empathy and cultural differences, which are not strengths of AI. Currently, 
human researchers are best suited to address these aspects, but synchronizing AI outputs 
with these insights—ensuring that AI does not lose subtle but important nuances— 
remains a signiﬁcant hurdle. Additionally, users have expressed concerns about data 
privacy, particularly in commercial environments where strict conﬁdentiality is empha-
sized. These ethical and regulatory concerns must be addressed to make AI tools more 
compliant with privacy standards. 
Looking ahead, there is potential for further reﬁnement. AI’s real-time, proactive 
involvement in user research could extend beyond asking interview questions and associ-
ating background knowledge. With advancements in agent technology, AI could include 
emotional recognition and become more attuned to users’ needs, thus fostering deeper 
collaboration. Furthermore, this research opens up broader discussions, such as how AI 
can take an active role in creative processes. For AI to be proactive, it must collect a wide 
range of information, including implicit and environmental data, to provide real-time 
outputs (e.g., listening to interview dialogues). The integration of ambient intelligence 
(AmI) and ubiquitous intelligence could unlock vast possibilities for AI to exercise 
creativity, requiring further exploration [65]. 
6.3 Conclusion 
This study highlights the collaborative creativity between humans and AI in HCD user 
research. Through formative research, we developed a design framework and require-
ments to guide the development of AI tools that facilitate active, real-time collabo-
ration. The AI co-researcher, based on this framework, was evaluated against passive 
AI-human collaboration tools. The results demonstrated that the AI co-researcher sig-
niﬁcantly enhanced creativity and provided valuable insights for future AI-driven tools. 
This work emphasizes the potential of AI agents to augment creativity and efﬁciency 
in user research workﬂows. While challenges remain, such as data overload and AI’s 
lack of empathy, integrating AI in ways that complement human insight and creativity 
can signiﬁcantly advance the ﬁeld. Future research should focus on reﬁning human-AI 
collaboration models and enhancing the adaptability of AI tools to speciﬁc user research 
needs.
