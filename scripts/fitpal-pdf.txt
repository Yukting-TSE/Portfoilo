ABSTRACT
Enhancing elderly physical health with regular exercise is key
in aging society research. However, in the absence of scientifi-
cally validated exercise guidelines, older adults are susceptible to
adopting excessive or inappropriate exercise methods that may
be detrimental to their health. In research related to the elderly’s
exercise conceptions, the potential for community-engaged inves-
tigations has been mentioned. However, no study has proposed
a community-based systematic design approach to reshape these
misconceptions. Our study closely examined six elderly individuals
inclined to exercise near their homes to research on their perception
on daily excercise. Then we propose a new design paradigm that
integrates an AI Chatbot, FitPal, for personalized exercise guidance
and community-driven incentives to correct misconceptions and
encourage long-term adherence to exercise among elders.

INTRODUCTION
Elderly individuals are characterized by a high incidence of diseases,
disability rates, and medical utilization. The health issues of the
elderly are poised to become a grave social concern in China[37].
Daily exercise plays a crucial role in maintaining the quality of
life and well-being of the elderly[33]. Current research indicates
that daily exercise can reduce all-cause mortality, lower the risk of
certain chronic illnesses, and maintain muscle mass[15, 21, 30, 36].
Many seniors opt for daily exercise as a means to preserve their
health[16, 31].
However, The elderly often engage in unscientific daily exer-
cise due to misconception of proper techniques[10, 19, 23], which
can be detrimental to their health[28]. They exercise according
to their own interpretations of health, believing it enhances their
physical condition and can treat or prevent both minor and se-
rious illnesses.[20]. The causes of these misconceptions include
the absence of professional daily exercise coaching[19], forgetting
or unwillingness to adhere to medical advice[1, 9], overreliance
on social media for health information[8], entrenched subjective
beliefs[20], and the high cost of learning professional daily exercise
knowledge[1].
In the field of research on the exercise conceptions of the elderly,
it has been noted that communities may potentially provide avenues
for older adults to access professional sports information[19]. How-
ever, no study has yet proposed a systematic design approach based
on communities to reshape their misconceptions. Research[35] on
AI chatbots that assist the elderly in acquiring health knowledge
has demonstrated advantages in delivering specialized information
to this demographic. The combination of community resources with
AI chatbots holds promise for redefining the unscientific exercise
perceptions among the elderly.
This leads us to our research questions(RQs):
* RQ1: What misconceptions do the elderly have about daily
exercise?
* RQ2: What are the opportunities community-based and AI
chatbot integrated design paradigms can provide to reshape
the elderly’s misconceptions about daily exercise?

To further understand the exercise misconceptions among the
elderly and to explore potential pathways for reshaping these mis-
conceptions, Our approach included literature review, participatory
observation, and in-depth interviews. Our work highlighted the
range of misconceptions that older adults possess during daily
exercise. These findings will direct researchers to focus on and com-
prehend the issues surrounding exercise misconceptions among
the elderly. Furthermore, we introduced a design paradigm that
integrates AI chatbots with community services. This paradigm
provides a reference and possibility for leveraging AI technology
in community care services to guide scientific daily exercise for the

RELATED WORK
2.1
Community Support for Elderly Exercise
Community-organized activities offer numerous benefits to the
physical health of the elderly. These benefits include enhancing
the enthusiasm of the elderly towards exercise[6, 13, 29], provid-
ing positive health feedback[34], reducing their dependency on
medications[5], and alleviating feelings of loneliness[14, 16]. Mod-
ern communities often provide the elderly with public exercise
facilities, group exercise activities, and exercise plans to facilitate
their engagement in physical activities. In Wuhan, there are self-
organized exercise organizations among the elderly to arrange and
manage their exercise activities[26]. The Shanghai exercise and
Medicine Integration Center offers public exercise and health ser-
vice facilities and spaces for the elderly, conducts health education
activities, and develops comprehensive exercise and health manage-
ment plans[5]. However, current community activities related to
elderly exercise still face challenges such as the lack of professional
exercise instructors, exercise activity management talent, and in-
sufficient promotion of the proper use of exercise equipment[7, 18].
Therefore, we aim to leverage the advantages of communities in the
field of elderly exercise to boost their enthusiasm for exercise and
encourage them to engage in more scientific and healthy physical
activities.
2.2
AI Support for Elderly Exercise
The advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) technology has sig-
nificantly benefited elderly healthcare, offering various types of
support such as emotional companionship, therapeutic assistance,
and medical supervision [22, 39]. AI chatbots are extensively used
in elderly healthcare to provide companionship [2] and supervision
[27]. For example, Miura et al. [27] designed an AI mobile chatbot
to offer healthcare advice. Gudala et al. [11] utilized AI chatbots
to remind elderly individuals to take their medications on time,
thereby improving their medication adherence [24]. Additionally,
these AI chatbots serve as a source of health knowledge for the
elderly [35].
In the field of elderly physical health, AI technologies also in-
troduce new opportunities. For exercise rehabilitation, AI-assisted
exercise programs have shown better outcomes for certain diseases
compared to traditional methods [12, 25]. In terms of active ex-
ercise, AI-powered smartwatch enhance exercise motivation [17].
These devices also provide more accurate medical prescriptions
[3, 4] and exercise feedback [38] for the elderly. However, issues of
trust and dependency arise when the elderly use AI. Some studies
[32] suggest improving the elderly’s acceptance of AI technology
by addressing trust transfer and operational simplicity. We believe
that AI technology has the potential to empower and guide the
elderly towards a scientific understanding of exercise. Clear meth-
ods of application are needed to provide professional and scientific
support for elderly exercise routines.

METHOD
In this study, we engaged with elderly residents in Shanghai com-
munities through observation and in-depth interviews to grasp
their perceptions of daily physical activity, the community’s influ-
ence on their daily exercise routines, and their receptiveness to
smart health devices.
3.1
Observation
Our study involved observations of elderly individuals engaging in
daily exercises at locations commonly frequented by this age group,
including residential areas, parks, care centers, and nursing homes.
We focused on the types of exercises they performed, their meth-
ods, and their use of community exercise facilities. Additionally,
we examined the community’s exercise atmosphere among the el-
derly. We also conducted participatory observations to understand
their social interactions during group activities in the community.
Our findings were documented through photographs and video
recordings.
3.2
Interview
We initiated casual conversations with elders at exercise venues
to identify individuals, aged 60 to 90, who regularly participate in
daily workouts. Upon their willingness to communicate with us,
we conducted in-depth interviews. A total of six elders participated
in the interviews, comprising an equal number of males (n=3) and
females (n=3). Details of these participants were summarized in
Table 1.

4
FINDINGS
4.1
Exercise Misconceptions Among Elders
4.1.1
Preference-Based Exercise Selection and Disregarding Scien-
tific Exercise Prescriptions. Elderly individuals often select exercises
based on personal preferences, neglecting the diversity and suit-
ability of exercise types. They tend to adhere to a single form of
exercise. For instance, one participant mentioned, "As I grow older,
I can’t do much else, so I just walk around daily" (P6), while another
said, "I love to dance; it keeps me free of worries" (P1). They seldom
consider their physical condition or the overall impact of their ac-
tivities, which can lead to the overuse of certain body parts, causing
injury and deviating from the original intention of maintaining
health through exercise.
Moreover, most elderly participants suffer from common health
issues such as arthritis, periarthritis, and back pain (P2, P3, P4).
Despite this, they often disregard medical advice when exercising
and instead create exercise plans based on personal understanding.
For example, one participant believed, "More exercise can cure dis-
eases without surgery" (P3), and another mentioned, "Doing pull-ups
can relieve back pain" (P4). This self-devised approach to exercise,
lacking scientific support, can exacerbate health conditions. One
participant even ignored professional guidance, stating, "The doctor
didn’t really say anything" (P5).
These findings show that the elderly need proper guidance and
education to correct their exercise habits. Professional advice can
help them exercise safely and effectively, promoting better physical
and mental health.
4.1.2
Ignoring Frequency and Method of Exercise. The elderly typi-
cally do not focus on the recommended frequency and method of
exercise. "I can’t stay seated at home, sometimes I even walk around at
midnight" (P2). "I injured my ligaments due to incorrect weightlifting
posture" (P4). The overestimation of their physical capabilities can
result in over-exercising, causing hidden physical injuries, under-
scoring the need for tailored guidance to help the elderly balance
their exercise routines and avoid potential harm.
4.2
Opportunities for Reshaping the Elderly’s
Misconceptions about Daily Exercise
4.2.1
Community Engagement in Encouraging Exercise. The prox-
imity and abundance of community exercise facilities significantly
motivated participants to engage in physical activities. "I live nearby
and there are plenty of facilities available, so I often come here to
exercise" (P4). Additionally, the positive exercise atmosphere with
in the community was highlighted, "The dance environment here
is fantastic, making it easy to make many friends" (P1). In such a
conducive environment, the elderly are more inclined to actively
participate in the daily excercise. Many elderly actively invite oth-
ers to join their exercise routines, providing mutual guidance and
fostering a supportive atmosphere. Community volunteers promote
scientifically effective ways of exercising, offering healthier options.
4.2.2
Acceptance of Smart Health Devices Among the Elderly. Our
research indicates that the majority of elderly individuals are famil-
iar with using smartphones and proficient in basic functions such
as making calls and using WeChat. Participant P3 mentioned, "I
often use my phone to chat with friends," while P1 stated, "I enjoy
watching dance videos shared by others." Some elderly individuals
also express keen interest in health data, such as the WeChat step
analyzer. When asked about health monitoring devices like smart-
watches, most elderly participants showed no resistance. Given the
acceptance and interest among the elderly, we believe that smart
health devices should be further utilized to help them accurately
assess their health status and cultivate healthy exercise habits.

DESIGN
5.1
Design Goals
To foster scientific exercise among the elderly, we established three
design objectives to guide our design process: First, our design
should convey scientific exercise knowledge in a manner that is
easily acceptable to the elderly, aiding them in understanding the
importance of scientific exercise. This understanding is intended to
correct their previous misconceptions and encourage more active
participation in scientific exercise routines. Second, professional
medical institutions should be involved to provide scientifically
tailored exercise prescriptions based on the physical condition of the
elderly. The system should guide the elderly in exercising according
to these prescriptions, while continuously monitoring their exercise
behavior to help them achieve a healthy physical state through
scientific exercise. Third, the system should motivate the elderly
to engage in scientific exercise, maintaining their enthusiasm and
initiative for long-term scientific exercise.
5.2
System Design
Based on the aforementioned goals, we propose the following ser-
vice system. The details of the system diagram are summarized in
Figure 1. Our system consists of three main components: User Side,
Server Side, and Community Side.
User Side includes the elderly, a fitness wristband, and an AI
chatbot. The elderly are required to wear the fitness wristband in
their daily lives to collect exercise data. The AI chatbot, named
FitPal, interacts with the elderly via the WeChat platform on their
mobile phones. The interactions include disseminating scientific
exercise knowledge, delivering exercise prescriptions, providing
feedback, and offering encouragement. FitPal, combining the mean-
ings of "Fit" and "Pal," represents a fitness partner designed to help
the elderly maintain a healthy, active lifestyle.
Community Side comprises community organizations, govern-
ment, medical institutions, and volunteers. The community collab-
orates with medical institutions to obtain health assessments and
exercise prescriptions for the elderly. The government provides
funding for material rewards such as food, fitness equipment, and
daily necessities. Volunteers manage and distribute these rewards
to the elderly who engage in scientific exercise, motivating them
to maintain healthy exercise habits.
Server Side is primarily responsible for receiving, processing,
and transmitting data. The server establishes a database based on
healthcare data, professional exercise prescriptions, and the initial
physical data of the elderly. It receives daily exercise data from the
fitness wristbands, updating the database accordingly. The server
transmits these resources to FitPal to be used as dialogue material.
Additionally, the elderly’s exercise data is sent to the community,
facilitating the community’s health management efforts.
Further details of the system will be elaborated in the following
sections.
5.2.1
AI-Enhanced User Interaction. We have designed a clear seam-
less interaction process to help elderly individuals maintain enthusi-
asm for exercise. We guide the elderly in scientific exercise through
FitPal, a personable voice chatbot. FitPal conveys scientific exercise
knowledge to the elderly, addresses their questions, and corrects
their misconceptions through voice interaction during exercise. It
also provides feedback on the scientific validity of the elderly’s
exercise afterward. FitPal uses GPT-4 and prompt engineering to
function as a query interface to the exercise prescription database
for the elderly. The system retrieves data on daily physical activity
and exercise prescriptions using Retrieval Augmented Generation
(RAG) for information indexing. The results, including answers
to questions, recommendations for diverse exercise methods, and
feedback on the scientific level of exercise, are communicated to
the elderly in an understandable manner via mobile devices. FitPal
intergrates customizable AI voice libraries to meet the social needs
of the elderly.Details of FitPal System prototype were summarized
in Figure 2.
5.2.2
Data Processing Section. We use exercise smartwatch as data
collection endpoint. These devices gather data on exercise dura-
tion, heart rate, steps, speed, and other related metrics. This data is
collected through built-in gyroscopes, heart rate sensors, accelerom-
eters, GPS modules and so on. The data collected by the smartwatch
are transmitted to mobile app front-ends via Bluetooth Low Energy
(BLE) APIs. The backend server collects this data to establish an
exercise prescription Q&A database, which includes initial physical

5.2.3
Community Service Endpoint. Community eldercare service
center staff promote the importance of scientific exercise to the

wear, activate, and monitor exercise intensity, and assist the elderly
in adding FitPal on WeChat. The community establishes a scientific
exercise ranking list based on the exercise prescription database,

munity eldercare service centers is reduced, decreasing government
expenditures on elderly care and promoting a sustainable cycle of
eldercare services.
6
LIMITATIONS
In this study, we employed a qualitative research method by con-
ducting in-depth interviews with six elderly individuals to explore
their perceptions of daily exercise. While this approach is valu-
able for gaining a deep understanding of the specific needs and
experiences of older adults, the small sample size and the focus on
particular individuals limit the generalizability and broad applica-
bility of our findings. Consequently, our study results may not fully
represent the perspectives and experiences of the wider elderly
population. Future research should aim to increase the sample size
and include a more diverse group of participants to enhance the
reliability and generalizability of the research conclusions.
Furthermore, despite current technological advancements, there
remains a possibility that AI systems could produce inappropriate or
erroneous outputs, which may negatively impact user experience. In
future work, we plan to improve the AI chatbot, FitPal, by leveraging
more advanced language models and optimized prompts to mitigate
the risk of generating unsuitable content and to better cater to the
needs of elderly users.
7
CONCLUSION
In this study, we have identified misconceptions among the elderly
regarding their choice of exercise, such as selecting types of exercise
based solely on personal preferences, disregarding the scientific
planning of exercise frequency and methods, and not following
doctors’ exercise recommendations. These behaviors could lead to
an increased risk of health issues. Consequently, we have proposed a
design solution that integrates an AI Chatbot excercise partner and
community support, aimed at transforming the elderly’s exercise
perceptions and encouraging the adoption of scientific exercise
methods. This initiative is designed to enhance their health levels
and alleviate the pressures on community eldercare service centers.
Our proposed solution holds promise for promoting the health and
well-being of the elderly and fostering the sustainable development
of eldercare services.
